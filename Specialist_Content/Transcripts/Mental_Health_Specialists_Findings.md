## 2. Key Findings & System Architecture Validation

### Barriers to Access & Demographics
* **Primary Barriers:** Social stigma or fear of judgment was identified as the most common barrier (60% of respondents), followed by poor awareness and financial constraints.
* **Language Handling:** Specialists handle language barriers variably; while some find it rarely an issue or are fluent themselves, others rely on assistants or refer patients to other specialists. 

### Telemedicine & Digital Accessibility
* **Comfort with Video Consultations:** Highly positive. 80% of the respondents rated their comfort level as 5/5 (Very Comfortable), and 20% rated it 4/5. 
* **Network Fallback (One-tap phone call):** Views are mixed. 60% are Neutral (patients usually just reconnect), while 40% consider it a Somewhat Critical backup.
* **Simplified "Adult Mode" for Elderly:** Essential features highlighted include *Easy access to the scheduled video link* (40%), *Direct appointment booking* (20%), *Emergency contact numbers* (20%), and *Basic mental health awareness resources* (20%).

### Appointment & Clinic Management
* **Centralized Scheduling:** 80% found a centralized system linking multiple locations to be either 'Somewhat Useful' or 'Highly Useful'.
* **Live Patient Queue Tracker:** Opinions are polarized. 40% found it Highly Beneficial to reduce confusion and crowding, 40% were Neutral, and 20% warned it might have a Negative impact by causing anxiety or pressure during consultations.

### Emergency Support & Patient Safety
* **Emergency Slots:** 80% do not currently set aside specific time slots for emergencies (either their schedule doesn't allow it, or they refer active emergencies to hospitals). Only 20% intentionally keep emergency slots open.
* **Crisis Direct Call:** 60% stated they do not handle active crises remotely. The remaining 40% are willing to accept direct calls either during specified working hours or exclusively for existing registered patients.
* **Live Location Sharing:** For sharing live locations during a crisis, a **Mandatory manual confirmation from the user before sharing** is the most highly recommended safeguard (60%), followed by restricting location data to verified helplines/responders.

### AI Triage & Volunteer Support
* **AI Red Flags:** Mentions of self-harm or suicidal thoughts (60%), severe trauma/abuse (20%), and substance overdose (20%) were highlighted as conversational red flags the AI should immediately escalate to emergency helplines.
* **Volunteer First-Aid Training:** Required training modules for the Volunteer Listener Program include *Active Listening & Empathy Training* (40%), *Suicide Prevention & Crisis Recognition* (40%), and *Maintaining Professional Boundaries & Ethics* (20%).
* **Anonymous Community Consultation:** Perspectives are highly mixed. 40% Strongly Support it, noting shared experiences are valuable. 20% Support conditionally (only if moderated), 20% are Neutral, and 20% Oppose it due to the high risk of medical misinformation.
